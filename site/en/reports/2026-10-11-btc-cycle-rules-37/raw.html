RISKRisk first: when this conclusion is void

The call in this report is that "the $58,525 close on June 30 is most likely the low of this bear market." The moment any daily close falls below $58,525, the call is void, and under our rules the state goes straight back to "Bear."

What if price drops to $60,000 but not below $58,525? By definition, the June 30 close is still this bear market's low, so the bottom still stands. Call it a "retest." But note that after the previous three confirmed bottoms, the lowest pullbacks stopped 21%, 56% and 28% above the low. None came this close ($60,000 would be only 2.5% above the low). If we got there, our confidence that "the bottom is behind us" would drop noticeably, and our daily reading would carry a "bottom in doubt" flag.

Even if the call is right, that does not mean price rises in a straight line. In the year after past confirmed bottoms, the maximum drawdown was 20%, 32% and 61% in the three cycles. Black swans can arrive at any time: size your positions as if another drop of 30% or more is still possible.

Three-minute summary
  1. The timing holds; the amplitude doesn't. From halving to cycle top, the last three cycles took 525 to 546 days, less than three weeks apart. But each cycle's top is less frenzied and each bottom less deep: this bear market fell only 53%, versus 77% to 85% in the previous three.
  2. Rules with "hard-coded numbers" are failing en masse. We back-tested 37 cycle rules, drawn from the wider market and from two Chinese-language on-chain analysts, Murphy (@Murphychen888) and CryptoChan (@0xCryptoChan). 15 have stopped working, mostly fixed thresholds like "the bottom is in once MVRV drops below 1" and countdowns like "the top comes X days after two lines cross."
  3. What survives are "where are we" rules, not "count the days" rules. For example, "this cycle's holder-cost lines have crossed, so the bottom is behind us." Six such signals this cycle point to June 30.
  4. Where we are now: late bear → early bull, bottom confirmed, day 46. The up-leg from June 30 is 103 days old and peaked at +48%. It has passed the "89-day line" we use in our daily Hot-Coin Analysis.
  5. Of the two calls in our August special, one has played out and one is still ahead. The $79,009 confirmation line has held. "Real acceleration waits until 15 months before the halving (mid-January 2027)" still has about 3 months to go.

What data we used

  • Period: July 2010 to October 2026, daily, about 5,900 days.
  • Price and valuation: Coin Metrics closing price, market cap, and realized price (the average cost of all coins).
  • On-chain holder data: bitview.space (the open-source Bitcoin Research Kit). It splits all bitcoin into 63 bands by how long since it last moved, and computes each band's average cost.
  • Analyst rules: we collected about 5,600 posts that Murphy and CryptoChan have published publicly on X and Telegram since 2021, extracted the rules that can be computed from data, and back-tested each one.
  • How we validated:
    • each rule is checked in each of the four cycles separately, not just pooled together;
    • we use only earlier cycles' numbers to predict the next cycle and see how accurate the forecast is;
    • we split by era (2014-17, 2018-21, 2022-26) to see whether a pattern holds, fades or reverses.
ConceptOut-of-sample test

Fix the rule using past data, then check it against a cycle that did not exist when the rule was set. Many rules look "right in all four cycles" simply because they were drawn from those four cycles. Only a hit on a cycle that played no part in setting the rule counts.

On the analysts quoted here: Some of the rules tested in this report come from public posts by two on-chain analysts, Murphy and CryptoChan, and are credited to them. Where we write that a rule has "stopped working," we mostly mean an old number no longer fits this cycle, not that the method itself is wrong. Here are their key dated calls of the past few years, checked against what happened:

01The rhythm holds: tops come about 530 days after the halving

ConceptHalving

Every 210,000 blocks (about four years), the new-coin reward miners receive per block is cut in half. The last four were Nov 2012, Jul 2016, May 2020 and Apr 2024; the next is expected in April 2028. The "four-year cycle" refers to the bull-and-bear loop that revolves around the halving.

We find each cycle's top and bottom from closing prices using a fixed rule, with no hand-picking. A cycle top is an all-time high followed by a fall of more than 50% that lasts at least 150 days. This rules out mid-bull-market crashes like April 2013 and April 2021.

Cycle Halving date Cycle top Days from halving to top Cycle bottom Bear-market drop Bear-market length
Cycle 1 2012-11-28 2013-12-04 371 days 2015-01-14 −84.5% 406 days
Cycle 2 2016-07-09 2017-12-16 525 days 2018-12-15 −83.8% 364 days
Cycle 3 2020-05-11 2021-11-08 546 days 2022-11-09 −76.7% 366 days
Cycle 4 (current) 2024-04-20 2025-10-06 534 days 2026-06-30 (so far) −53.1% 267 days
  • Stable: from halving to top, the last three cycles took 525 to 546 days. Using only the previous cycle's day count to predict the next, the miss was 21 days for Cycle 3 and 12 days for this cycle. This is the most robust regularity in the whole study.
  • Unstable: bear-market length. The first three lasted about a year; this one reached its low in 267 days. Waiting for a bottom on a "bear markets last a year" basis would have meant arriving more than 3 months late.
What it means for you The most reliable thing about the cycle is roughly when the top comes, not how long the bear market lasts.

02The amplitude is shrinking: tops less frenzied, bottoms less deep

ConceptRealized price (average cost of all coins)

Each bitcoin is valued at the price when it last moved on-chain, and those values are averaged. Think of it as "the average buy price of all holders."

ConceptMVRV

Price ÷ realized price. At 2, the average holder is up 100%; below 1, the average holder is underwater.

Cycle MVRV at the bull-market peak MVRV at the bear-market trough Bear-market drop
Cycle 1 5.88 0.56 −84.5%
Cycle 2 4.72 0.69 −83.8%
Cycle 3 3.96 0.75 −76.7%
Cycle 4 (current) 2.74 1.10 −53.1%
  • At their hottest, valuations reached only 69% to 84% of the previous cycle's peak. At their coldest, valuations were higher each cycle.
  • This is the first time in Bitcoin's history that MVRV stayed above 1 through an entire bear market: the average holder was never underwater overall.
  • So sayings like "the bottom is in only once MVRV falls below 1" and "MVRV above 3.5 means the top" never triggered this cycle. The bottom came anyway, and so did the top.

Why this happens: the market is bigger, with ETFs, public companies and more long-term holders, so each cycle's mania and panic is weaker than the last. The logic still holds (expensive means risk, cheap means opportunity), but the numbers for "how expensive" and "how cheap" must be recalculated with each cycle.

03The 37-rule audit: what stays, what breaks

We grouped the 37 rules by type:

Type What it tries to answer Example Result
Bottom zone Are we near a bottom? More than half of coins at a loss; realized losses exceeding realized profits over the past year; price below the 200-week moving average Appeared near all four bottoms, so direction holds. But it shows up one to five months early and cannot date the bottom
Price floor How low can it go? CVDD Closing price has never gone below it since 2011, so it holds. But it sits far below price and works only as a last line of defence
Bottom confirmation Has the bottom passed? Cost lines of different holder-age groups crossing; price reclaiming several short-term cost lines at once Historically appeared 1 to 5 months around the real bottom, so direction holds. This cycle, 6 of them point to June 30
Fixed thresholds A number that marks the top or bottom MVRV below 1 is the bottom; one group's share of supply reaching 22% is the bottom Fail from Cycle 3 or this cycle on
Countdowns How many days to the top or bottom "The top comes 648 to 694 days after two lines cross" Projecting from earlier cycles to the next, most miss the window
Concept200-week moving average

The average closing price over the past 1,400 days. Price has rarely fallen below it; when it does, it is usually the deepest part of a bear market.

ConceptSupply in profit

The share of all bitcoin whose cost is below the current price, meaning coins still in profit. Below 50% means more than half of all coins are at a loss.

ConceptRealized profit / loss

When a coin is sold, the selling price minus the purchase price is a realized profit if positive and a realized loss if negative. "Losses exceeding profits over the past year" means the market is selling at a loss, or capitulating.

ConceptCVDD

A slow-moving line derived from the cumulative value of old coins when they are spent. Bitcoin's closing price has never fallen below it since data began, so it is often treated as a price floor. It currently sits around $46,000.

Why countdowns keep failing

The day ranges in these rules were all drawn by looking at the past three cycles. With three points you can always find a range that "just fits" all of them; apply it to a fourth cycle and it no longer does. A few examples:

Rule What it says What happened
After realized profit over the past year overtakes losses, days until the bull top 648 to 694 days 450 / 663 / 856 / 693 days: only two of four hit
After long-term holders turn profitable, months until the major bull top about 24 months 26-29 / 31 / 35-40 months
After the share of coins held 6 to 12 months peaks, days until the top 352 to 354 days 551 / 353 / 0 / 83 days
Days after the halving for the short-term heat gauge RHODL to peak 522 to 533 days This cycle it peaked 221 days after the halving, 10 months before the price top

One more problem is easy to miss: some rules target a "recovery-phase top" or a "mini top," and where such a top sits is often decided only in hindsight. If the target can be chosen, the rule can never be wrong. In our back-tests we fixed the target as "the next swing high followed by a drop of more than 15%," so our results will not exactly match the day counts the authors themselves cite.

What it means for you When you see a forecast precise to "day X," ask first: how many cycles was this range drawn from? Was it checked against a cycle that played no part in setting it?

04The eight questions retail investors ask most

Q1 One-year bearQ2 MVRV below 1Q3 Valuation topsQ4 Halving overlaysQ5 Miner costQ6 Long-term holdersQ7 Up 40%Q8 Next top

Q1Do bear markets bottom after about a year?

COMMON CLAIMThe first three all took about a year, so just wait a year after the top.

What the data saysThis cycle broke it. Top to bottom took 406, 364 and 366 days in the first three; this cycle took 267. Projecting from the previous cycle misses this bottom by 99 days.

How to use it Judge the end of a bear market by "state" (Section 05), not by counting days.

Q2Is the real bottom only when MVRV falls below 1?

COMMON CLAIMWhen price falls below the average cost, everyone is underwater, and that is the true bottom.

What the data saysIt held in the first three cycles and failed this cycle.

  • In the first three cycles MVRV did fall below 1, but early: 102, 26 and 149 days ahead of the real bottom, when price was still 87%, 51% and 42% above the final low.
  • This cycle's lowest was 1.10; it never fell below 1. Anyone waiting for it would have missed the entire bottom.
How to use it Use MVRV to see how far price is from the average cost. Historically, buying when MVRV was below 1 made money over the following year 98% to 100% of the time. The undervalued zone still works; it just never showed up this cycle.

Q3Can valuation indicators still call the top?

COMMON CLAIMMVRV above 3.5, or the two moving averages of the Pi Cycle indicator crossing, marks the top.

ConceptPi Cycle indicator

A cycle-top signal that fires when the 111-day moving average crosses above twice the 350-day moving average.

What the data saysThey missed the last two cycles.

  • In the first two cycles, the valuation peak and the price peak came at about the same time.
  • In the last two, the valuation peak came 8 to 10 months before the price top. This cycle MVRV peaked at 2.74 in November 2024; on the day price topped in October 2025, MVRV was only 2.29.
  • Pi Cycle: in the first two cycles it called the true top to within 1 day; in 2021 it caught the April interim top, 7 months before the real one; this cycle it never crossed.
How to use it Valuation can still tell you whether things are expensive right now. Since 2018, buying with MVRV between 2 and 2.5 produced a negative median return over the next year. But "how expensive means the top" can no longer use the old numbers.

Q4Can halving overlays predict what comes next?

COMMON CLAIMAlign each cycle by halving date; whatever day count this cycle is on, look at what the previous cycle did next.

What the data saysThey are good for the big turning points only, not for the path. The overlays look alike mainly because everything trends up over the long run. Using 30-day returns instead, the cycles are almost uncorrelated; this cycle against the last is −0.02, meaning no relationship.

How to use it Use the overlay to see roughly what season of the cycle we are in, not to predict whether next month is up or down.

Q5Is the miner cost (electricity-cost line) the bottom?

COMMON CLAIMWhen price falls to miners' production cost, miners stop selling, and that is the bottom.

What the data saysIt is not a fixed bottom. We estimated miners' blended cost from difficulty. At the four real bottoms, price was 0.22, 0.42, 0.80 and 1.58 times that cost. In the first three cycles price spent months below the cost line; this cycle it never came near.

How to use it Price approaching miner cost signals miner stress and is a useful reference, but "once it gets there it's the bottom" does not hold.

Q6Are long-term holders selling? Is the top near?

ConceptShort-term / long-term holders

A coin that has not moved on-chain for more than about 155 days (5 months) counts as held by a "long-term holder"; otherwise it is held by a "short-term holder."

COMMON CLAIMLong-term holders starting to distribute marks the top; long-term holders selling at a loss marks the bottom.

What the data saysBoth are too crude.

  • "Long-term holders start distributing" has no fixed timing. We looked at the day long-term holders held the most coins; after it, they become net sellers. When that day fell:
Cycle The day long-term holders held the most coins, relative to the halving
Cycle 1 326 days after the halving
Cycle 2 179 days before the halving
Cycle 3 160 days after the halving
Cycle 4 (current) 139 days before the halving

Some began selling half a year before the halving, others nearly a year after; the timing simply does not line up. A claim like "heavy distribution starts about 160 days after the halving" fits only one of the four cycles.

  • "Long-term holders selling at a loss = bottom": it has occurred 47 times since 2011, repeatedly during bear markets. It only tells you "we're in a bear market," not where the bottom is.
How to use it What is useful is how hard they sell. After the bottoms of the first three bear markets, long-term holders each had one burst of capitulation. This cycle's burst came on June 30 itself, but it was the mildest in history, consistent with "the bottom is not as deep."

Q7Once price is up 40%, is the bottom safe?

COMMON CLAIMA rebound of 40% or more off the low confirms the bottom.

What the data saysMostly yes, with one important exception.

  • The rule: price is up 40% from the lowest close to date, and that low is at least 90 days old.
  • It has triggered 4 times and been right 3 times. The one miss was July 2014: after rising 40%, price fell below the prior low and dropped another 72% before the real bottom.
  • This cycle it triggered on September 28.
How to use it Bottom confirmation means "this cycle's low has most likely passed," not "price rises in a straight line from here." In the year after a confirmed bottom, Cycle 2 had a 61% drawdown (June 2019 to the March 2020 pandemic crash), Cycle 1 had 32% and Cycle 3 had 20%.

Q8Where is the next top?

What the data saysOnly an order of magnitude, not a date.

  • Timing: if "top 525 to 546 days after the halving" keeps holding, and the next halving is expected in April 2028, the next top would fall around September to October 2029. That rests on only 3 samples.
  • Height: extrapolating from the shrinking ratio of the last three valuation tops, the next cycle's peak MVRV might reach only 1.9 to 2.3. Again only 3 ratios, so treat it as an order of magnitude.
How to use it Over the medium term, the next top's time window is still far away. For short-term moves the cycle layer cannot answer; look at macro (Special #2) and holder structure (next issue).

05Where we are in the cycle now

We combined the rules that passed testing into six states

We use only the three kinds of signals that passed the tests above: bottom zone, bottom confirmation and top risk. Countdowns and fixed thresholds that have failed are not used. Each day is judged using only that day's and earlier data, with no peeking at the future.

State In plain English Entry condition
Bull Making new highs Close at an all-time high
Bull · top-risk zone Late bull market, be careful 480 to 600 days after the halving; or valuation and price are both already very high (MVRV at or above 2, and price at least 2× the 200-week moving average)
Bull pullback A big pullback inside a bull market, not yet confirmed as a bear Down 20% or more from the high
Bear Bear market Down 35% or more, more than 90 days since the high, and more than 450 days since the halving
Bear bottom zone The bottom area of a bear market In a bear market, at least 2 of the 6 bottom-zone signals are lit
Late bear → early bull The bottom has passed Up 40% from the low and the low is more than 90 days old; or price holds above four short-term cost lines for 7 straight days and a bottom confirmation such as a holder-age cost-line crossing has occurred

Replaying history to test it: we ran the rules over every day since 2012 and looked at how the following year went after each state.

State Median return over the next year Share of the time it made money Worst case
Late bear → early bull +152% 92% −69%

(2014 to 2026 combined. Adjacent days overlap heavily and there are only a handful of truly independent stretches, so look at direction only.)

  • On the day of each of the four true tops, the rule gave "top-risk zone";
  • the first entry into "bear bottom zone" came 1 to 5 months before the real bottom;
  • "bottom confirmation" came 2 to 5 months after the real bottom. It does not catch the lowest point; it catches that the lowest point has passed.

Known misses, stated plainly:

  • A false bottom confirmation in July 2014, after which price broke below the prior low and went back to Bear.
  • A 75-day false bear in August to October 2021: after falling 53% from the April high, it was classed as a bear and only turned back in mid-October; the very next day price made a new high. The market at the time was also widely calling a bear, and the rule could not tell the difference either.
  • These thresholds were set within this research, which used these same cycles, so the replay above is "in-sample." The real test starts now: the website records the state every day, and we check it against what happens.

This cycle's bottom-confirmation signals

ConceptAge-band cost lines

The average cost of only those coins that last moved within a given age range. For example, the "6-12 month cost" is the average cost of those who bought 6 to 12 months ago and have not moved since. In a bear market, the newer the buyer, the lower the cost, so around the bottom these lines cross in sequence.

ConceptShort-term holder cost basis

The average cost of those who bought in the last 5 months or so. Price above it means recent entrants are, on the whole, in profit.

ConceptTrue market mean

The average cost of only the capital still actively trading, excluding coins untouched for years and possibly lost.

Signal How far from the real bottom, historically This cycle

Long-term holder capitulation peaks 12 to 44 days after the bottom June 30 itself
On-chain turnover speed (SLRV) at its low 17 to 69 days before the bottom July 5 (5 days after the bottom)
Three age-band cost lines cross in turn (the "triple cross") Both crosses within 85 days of the bottom June 30 falls between the second and third cross
Price above four short-term cost lines at once 77 to 177 days after the bottom August 27 (58 days after the bottom)
Balanced price falls below long-term holder cost, then bottoms 102 to 185 days later 131 days later

Counter-evidence, shown too:

  • The classic signal "short-term holder cost falls below long-term holder cost" did not occur this cycle. The reason is that the bear market was so shallow the two lines never came close.
  • The "losses exceeding profits over the past year" signal has historically appeared within 44 days of the real bottom; this cycle it only appeared on September 15, 77 days late.

The up-leg since June 30

ConceptUp-leg / down-leg

Cut the price path into segments, switching direction whenever price reverses by more than 10%. An up segment is an up-leg and a down segment is a down-leg; as long as the pullback inside a leg stays under 10%, it is still the same leg. This is also the convention used in our daily Hot-Coin Analysis.

Counting that way:

  • This bear market had only 4 down-legs: 124,824 → 84,775; 97,044 → 63,495; 74,724 → 65,967; 82,257 → 58,525 (with a rebound between each). The four earlier bear markets (2011, 2013-15, 2017-18, 2021-22) had 12, 15, 11 and 10 down-legs. Shallow and fast, which is the same thing as "the amplitude is shrinking."
  • The stretch from June 30 to now is a single up-leg, and it has not broken:
Item Reading
Start 2026-06-30, 58,525
Leg age 103 days
High about 86,500 on September 21 (+47.8%)
Max drawdown inside the leg 6.9% (September 15); under 10%, so the leg has not broken
Now 83,084 (+42.0%, −4.0% from the high)

The 89-day line: one alert in our daily Hot-Coin Analysis says that once an up-leg has run 89 days, it is "reduce only, no adding." The basis: of the 5 up-legs since 2019 that ran 89 days, all topped within 2 to 38 days afterwards. The gains remaining after day 89 were +39.7%, +0.9%, +7.4%, +7.7% and +12.9%, with drawdowns of 11% to 19% after the top. This leg reached day 89 on September 27, and its high (September 21) came before day 89.

Note: the 89-day line does not hold across all data since 2010; it held in all 5 cases only since 2019. We treat it as an alert, not a law.

What it means for you The cycle layer says the bottom has passed; the up-leg says this stretch has already run a while. The two do not contradict. The broad direction is up, but do not chase here. Wait for a pullback of 10% or more in this leg and a new leg to begin before considering adding.

INVALIDATIONWhen this call is void

  • A daily close below 58,525 → "bottom confirmed" is void and the state returns to Bear. Historically, in the "Bear" state, the median return over the following year was −36%, and it made money only 31% of the time.
  • An earlier warning: a close back below the short-term holder cost basis (about 74,000) and the true market mean (about 77,000) means recent entrants are, on the whole, underwater. This does not change the state, but it calls for more caution.
  • "Bottom in doubt": a close back below the 200-week moving average (about 66,000, roughly 13% above the low). After the previous three confirmed bottoms, the deepest pullbacks stopped 21% to 56% above the low; falling to this level would already be closer to the bottom than any of them. By the rules June 30 would still be the low, but the "bottom" would look more like a support being tested again and again than a starting point left far behind. (This one is a judgment call, not a rule measured from data.)
  • About "wicks": all price levels in this report are based on daily closes; intraday spikes do not count. The 58,525 on June 30 is a closing price, not a single wick. Likewise, if price dips below 58,525 intraday in future but closes back above it, that does not count as a break.
  • Black swans ignore rules: on March 12, 2020, the pandemic crash, Bitcoin closed down 37% in a single day, nearly a year after the Cycle 2 bottom had been confirmed, and it still never broke the 2018 low. Even if the bottom is real, it can first be driven through a psychological level. So size positions to withstand a drawdown of more than 30%.

Still standing (in direction only; just 4 cycles):

  • tops come 525 to 546 days after the halving;
  • the undervalued zone (MVRV below 1, price below 1.5× the 200-week moving average) is nearly always followed by a gain over the next year;
  • since 2018, the median return over the next year after overvalued zones is negative;
  • the closing price has never fallen below CVDD;
  • cost-line crossings between holder-age groups can confirm a bottom 1 to 5 months after it.

Limitations and disclaimers

  • There are only 4 independent cycle samples. Every conclusion tops out at "direction holds," not a law.
